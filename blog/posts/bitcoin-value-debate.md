# Bitcoin Value Debate: Proof-of-Work and Network Effects

> **Context Question**: Why do people say Bitcoin is the greater fool's theory?

## Original Comment

Because this has happened before, it's not too difficult to understand their position, all you have to do is to look at everything except well regulated developed country markets. Anything that comes with a lofty promise eventually ends up being a rug-pull. So they are on sound ground in rejecting something super intangible like Bitcoin, hell to them even tulip bulbs produced more tangible results.

The barrier that I personally had to overcome is understanding that the entirety of proof-of-work, all transactions are embedded in bitcoin-blockchain at-the-moment. This is **cryptographically verifiable**. Another realization needed is that the silicon & energy resources needed to perform that work is also embodied in current blockchain. It also represents the value of what could have been done using those resources, that is after all the value of anything. This value is real & it stands by itself.

So there is value embodied in Bitcoin itself. Now the question is how much? To that my answer is it's about **network effects**, current and past. It's not easy to replace the value formed organically in all players' minds by something that starting afresh. It's just not, otherwise we'll be creating new Google, Facebook & WhatsApp every year with better tech. And this is just current network effect. There is also past network effect embodied in bitcoin that I think is akin to Karma. This represents all the past interactions between current and (now) dormant players. This is like preserving the entire history of all currency transfers. You are not recreating that collective value that exists across all participants' minds anywhere else with a fresh new network no matter how hard you decree.

Sure today all Central banks can issue their own CBDCs but even if they do that they still can't devalue the Bitcoin anymore. In fact (my hunch) is that likely more they try, more they'll end up validating ("Streisand"-ing?!) it and accelerating it. AFAIU the only real way to kill bitcoin was to choke it at on/off ramps to fiat when it was young. That fact that we are $2.4T cap now and that we were instrumental in swinging a major election (crypto-bros in swing states!) tells me that ship has long sailed.

So what's left, slow embrace by trailing currencies. I guess we are in early stages of that. So buckle-up!

## Initial Critique

### Strong Points

**Proof-of-work insight**: Correctly identifies that computational work and energy are irreversibly embedded in the blockchain. This is a legitimate value argument.

**Network effects reasoning**: Valid analogy to Google/Facebook—established networks have moat advantages. The distinction between current and historical network effects is thoughtful.

### Weaknesses

#### 1. Circular logic on value

"Silicon & energy resources... that is after all the value of anything" conflates cost with value. Sunk costs don't determine market value. If I burn $1000 of electricity mining a token nobody wants, that energy expenditure doesn't magically create $1000 of value.

#### 2. "Past network effect" as karma

This is metaphysical hand-waving. Transaction history has archival value, but calling it "karma" that can't be recreated is mysticism dressed as economics. Ethereum forked and retained value. Networks can migrate.

#### 3. Election impact overclaimed

Crypto's electoral impact was marginal and heavily debated. Crypto *donors* spent $119M, but whether crypto *voters* were decisive is disputed. Claiming "we were instrumental" overstates evidence.

#### 4. CBDC strawman

"CBDCs can't devalue Bitcoin anymore" misunderstands the threat. CBDCs could reduce Bitcoin's utility as payment rail, not by "devaluing" it cryptographically but by offering superior UX/integration with zero volatility.

#### 5. Inevitability fallacy

"Ship has sailed" + "slow embrace by trailing currencies" assumes linear adoption. $4T market cap ≠ inevitable global reserve currency status. MySpace hit $12B valuation before Facebook. Market cap is not destiny.

#### 6. Missing counterarguments

- Energy cost as liability, not pure asset
- Regulatory risk beyond on/off ramps
- Deflationary design problems for currency use
- Concentration risk (whales own disproportionate supply)

### Verdict

Decent intuition on computational work and network effects, but reasoning jumps from "has some embedded value" to "inevitable global currency" without rigorous support. Overconfident given actual election data and regulatory uncertainty.

## Counter-Analysis

### The Gold Analogy Works... and Reveals the Problem

**You're correct**: Gold's value *does* partially derive from extraction costs creating scarcity. The energy/labor embedded in mining gold contributes to its value proposition—you can't trivially create more.

But here's the key difference:

**Gold has independent utility**:
- Industrial applications (electronics, aerospace)
- Jewelry/ornamentation (cultural/aesthetic value)
- Physical properties (non-corroding, malleable, conducts heat)

Even if everyone stopped valuing gold as money tomorrow, it would still have floors of value from these uses.

**Bitcoin's utility is purely as a ledger/currency**:
- If the network effect collapses, there's no "floor" of industrial demand
- The energy spent doesn't create physical properties
- The scarcity only matters if people coordinate on valuing it

### The Circularity Still Exists (But It's Not Fatal)

The logic is:
1. Energy spent → Hard to replicate → Scarcity
2. Scarcity → People value it → Network effect
3. Network effect → Justified to spend more energy → Back to step 1

This *is* circular, but so is all money. Fiat currency has the same problem (we value dollars because others do). The question is: **What breaks the circle?**

For fiat: Government backing, tax obligations, legal tender laws
For gold: Physical utility + millennia of coordination
For Bitcoin: Pure network effect + first-mover advantage in decentralized digital scarcity

### Your Karma/History Argument is Stronger Than Critics Allow

The "past network effect" isn't mysticism—it's path dependence. Consider:

**Why can't a new blockchain replace Bitcoin?**
- It would need to convince exchanges, miners, holders, merchants to switch
- Switching costs are real (infrastructure, liquidity, brand recognition)
- Even if technically superior, coordination is expensive

This is Schelling point dynamics, not karma. Bitcoin became the focal point for "digital gold" early, and that's hard to dislodge. You're right that this has value.

### Where I Think You're Overconfident

**"Ship has sailed" on regulatory chokepoints**

- While Bitcoin survived early attacks, regulation is still evolving
- Future scenarios: Custody crackdowns, mining bans, KYC requirements could still marginalize it
- $2.4T cap doesn't guarantee immunity from coordinated state action

**Election impact**

- Crypto's influence in 2024 was real but not decisive
- Claiming "we swung the election" conflates donor influence with voter behavior
- Most voters prioritize inflation/immigration; crypto was niche

**CBDC threat underestimated**

- You're right CBDCs can't "devalue" Bitcoin cryptographically
- But they could make it irrelevant for payments (which hurts the "digital currency" narrative)
- If Bitcoin becomes "just" a speculative asset, that's a much narrower use case

## Synthesis: Where We Agree

✅ Proof-of-work creates real scarcity
✅ Network effects are powerful and path-dependent
✅ Bitcoin has survived enough to be "hard to kill"
✅ Early regulatory chokepoints are weaker now

## Where the Debate Remains

❓ Does embedded energy = intrinsic value, or just proof-of-work?
❓ Is $2.4T cap "too big to fail" or vulnerable to black swan events?
❓ Will Bitcoin be digital gold, payment rail, or speculative niche?

---

## Final Take

Your core insight—that Bitcoin's value comes from verifiable work + network effects—is sound. The gold analogy is apt but incomplete (gold has non-monetary floors, Bitcoin doesn't).

The real question isn't *whether* Bitcoin has value (it clearly does via network effect), but *how stable* that value is and *what role* it will play. "Digital gold" is plausible. "Global reserve currency" requires much more proof.

The ship hasn't sailed on regulation or adoption—we're mid-voyage, and the destination is still uncertain.
